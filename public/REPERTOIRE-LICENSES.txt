BLUE HOUR — REPERTOIRE PROVENANCE AND ADAPTATION NOTES
核验日期 / Sources checked: 2026-10-12

范围 / Scope
The library contains 239 different playable tunes: 235 licensed chord-annotation
arrangements plus four early instrumental melodies. Take Five is listening-only.
Autumn Leaves and So What retain their listening recommendations and now also
have playable chord annotations; they are counted once each, not as duplicates.

Four early instrumental teaching arrangements are bundled in lib/repertoire.ts.
The additional data and mappings are in lib/standard-catalog-data.ts and
lib/standard-catalog.ts. Source melody is present only for the four Freed Jazz
tunes named below. For all other tunes, the automatic saxophone is a generated
chord-tone demonstration, not the tune's melody or a master solo transcription.
No lyrics, commercial recording audio, artist solo transcription, album artwork,
or Real Book scan is bundled. All sound in the game is synthesized by the app.
The original exercises elsewhere in the app are separate from these standards.

权利依据 / Rights basis
Freed Jazz publishes lead sheets for compositions it identifies as public domain
in the United States and declares its own editions public domain there:
https://freedjazz.org/
https://github.com/richardjs/freed-jazz
Its individual tune pages explicitly warn that copyright status can differ in
other territories. This is a US-based source declaration, not a worldwide license
or a claim that all jazz standards are open source. Local public distribution
requires checking the relevant territory. No rights in a recommended commercial
recording are obtained through the status of the underlying composition.
US Copyright Office explanation of separate musical-work and recording rights:
https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ56a.pdf

教学改编 / Adaptation
- Read the melody staff in the source MuseScore files. Preserve written pitch,
  note duration, dotted rhythm, tuplets, rests and ties as timed MIDI note events.
  Do not include the lyrics or editorial layout.
- Use one chord per 4/4 bar: retain the FIRST source chord in that bar and hold it
  for the bar. Omit later changes within the bar. This is a deliberately simpler
  harmonic accompaniment, not a claim to reproduce the entire source arrangement.
- Normalize source chord spelling to the app's grammar (e.g. -7 to m7, ^ to maj7,
  07 to m7b5, o7 to dim7); preserve slash-bass notes. No unsupported symbol is
  silently substituted at playback.
- Oh, Lady Be Good!, Look for the Silver Lining and Embraceable You use all 32
  bars of the source theme. I Got Rhythm uses the first 32 bars of its 36 written
  source bars: repeat/coda navigation and the four written coda bars are omitted.
  The app labels this as a 32-bar learning loop, not the complete original ending.
- Game tempo, swing feel, instrument sounds, voicings, bass lines and drum patterns
  are the app's teaching arrangement. They do not recreate any linked artist's
  performance. Playback swing can change the feel of the source's written rhythm.
- Imported files and this source corpus are different pathways. There is no
  automated importer or implied permission for a complete Real Book collection.

Selection and transformation
- Start from 2,048 published playlist JAMS annotations, 1,866 with explicit 4/4.
- Match a curated list of jazz-standard titles, yielding 311 different names.
  Non-4/4, unknown-metre and transposed duplicate entries are not counted.
- Remove N/no-chord entries and annotations with fractional equal divisions that
  require a separate rhythmic interpretation. Keep only continuous integer-beat
  annotation sequences supported by the current teaching engine.
- Exclude seven charts with unsupported chord qualities rather than silently
  replacing those chords. Also hold back I'll Remember April and Cherokee for
  a separate review of their expanded-form length.
- The resulting selection has 235 unique titles. The fixed list of source IDs and
  SHA-256 checksums is stored per record in lib/standard-catalog-data.ts. Each UI
  card links directly to its own JAMS file at the pinned source revision.
- Preserve the source's expanded chord order and every retained within-bar change.
  The source may contain repetitions, introductions or endings. It is a specific
  annotated arrangement, not a guarantee of the canonical form of every tune.
- Convert ChoCo's score-time encoding to absolute quarter-note beats:
  (floor(sourceTime) - 1) * 4 + 10 * fractionalPart(sourceTime).
  Preserve durations and assert continuous complete 4/4 bars.
- Normalize equivalent chord spelling, not harmonic content: -7 -> m7,
  ^/^7 -> maj7, h/h7 -> m7b5, o7 -> dim7, o -> dim, -^7 -> mMaj7,
  7at -> 7alt, 69 -> 6/9, etc. These aliases are defined in ChoCo's grammar:
  https://github.com/smashub/choco/blob/5fe168fd55be5c84512abcfbc4e6f1b1f8f0092a/choco/converters/grammar/leadsheet/leadsheet_ireal.lark
- Source credits and style labels are retained. The source parser populates its
  artist metadata field from tune.composer, but this project still labels it
  "source credit" rather than claiming each composer's identity was independently
  researched. Publication years are not supplied by the dataset and are omitted.
- Teaching tempo and synthesized accompaniment are chosen by this app. The style
  label is source metadata; the engine does not recreate every named historical
  style or recording. Automatic saxophone lines are generated chord-tone examples.

Reproduction
Clone the source repository and check out the pinned revision, then run:
  python3 lib/standard-catalog-generate.py /path/to/choco
The script reads the fixed source-ID selection in standard-catalog-data.ts and
rebuilds the dataset. It verifies the commit, metre, contiguous timing, chord
spelling and unique titles before writing. It does not retrieve commercial media.

Validation
The corpus tests assert at least 220 unique selectable playable standards, valid
harmony and beat boundaries, preservation through the app's import validator,
per-tune attribution/checksums, and retention of the original four melodies and
14 verified recording links. These are data-integrity checks; they do not claim
music-teacher review of every annotation or measured learner improvement.
